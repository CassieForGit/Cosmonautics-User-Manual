## Basic Aerodynamics

In Aerodynamics you have to account for the four forces of newton (discussed in Physics Basic Principles) to control a body in the air or by it's means.

To make for example a plane to fly the Lift force of the plane has to be higher than the Weight force of the plane.
This is done by increasing the relative velocity between the plane and the air and, while increasing drag, increases lift.
The relative velocity has to be high in order to keep the plane flying because if it went any slower the lift would be lower than the weight and the plane would enter what's called a "Stall".

A plane produces all that lift not only by the velocity but also by it's surface area pointing towards it's speed direction. The surface area is enough to lift the plane up thanks to the wings.

A plane controls where it's pointing at (called "Attitude") via control wings. The control wings use drag to steer the plane. If more drag is produced to the left relative to the Center of Mass then the plane will rotate towards the right since it'll be easier for it to steer in that way, and vice versa.

A plane has to be stable. The Center of pressure has to be behind the Center of Mass (relative to the Vector of speed). Think of it as the pendulum effect, it's easier to hold an object from it's tip rather than one point from it's base.
